# LIFE prototype: member usability check

Purpose: confirm that the experience gives members a clear starting point without requiring them to understand the financial machinery. A code review cannot establish a 10/10 member experience.

Use five to eight representative members initially, including a single person, a couple, a renter, someone approaching retirement and someone already retired. Include mobile users and someone who uses enlarged text or assistive technology. Use fictional figures if they prefer. Do not collect financial information through the prototype; inputs stay in the page. Do not coach participants through the tasks.

## Tasks

1. Enter your circumstances (or the example values) and complete the six questions.
2. Explain the starting point in your own words. What makes it fit, or not fit, your priorities?
3. Explore greater income certainty. What changed? What did you gain and give up?
4. Explore enjoying more early retirement and a larger safety buffer. Explain the combined result.
5. Change a balance or retirement age, return to your results, then review and save your approach.

## Five questions to validate

- Can members answer the inputs without help? Record confusing terms, defaults mistaken for facts and questions they cannot answer.
- Does the starting point feel credible? Can they connect at least two of their answers to the reserve, lifestyle, future or lifetime purposes?
- Do they understand LIFE as purposes rather than four bank accounts? Can they distinguish the emergency reserve from the larger accessible-savings figure?
- Can they describe both sides of the income-certainty and early-spending trade-offs? Can they distinguish lifetime-product income from means-tested Age Pension and avoid adding the pension to spending twice?
- Do they finish clearer about where to start? Ask before and after: “How clear do you feel about a starting point for retirement?” (0–10), then ask what remains unclear. This rating is exploratory, not a validated anxiety scale.

## Record and iterate

Record task completion, points of hesitation, misunderstandings, participant explanations and before/after clarity. Treat any repeated misconception about income, access to savings or the model's certainty as a blocker. Fix those issues and retest. Report the actual observations and limitations rather than awarding a perfect score from a developer review.

## Manual device/accessibility checks

Check at 320, 390, 768 and 1280 CSS-pixel widths; portrait and landscape; 200% text enlargement; keyboard-only navigation; and a screen reader. Check that controls and feedback remain visible, the dialog is named and restores focus on close, radio arrow keys work, changes are announced without overwhelming narration, and horizontal scrolling is limited to optional tabular details. Native controls and source checks support these checks but do not replace them.
